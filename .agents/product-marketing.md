# Product marketing context — Acme Robotics (pilot)

Seeded for scheduled agent runs. Skills that would normally "ask the user" should read this file plus `config/pilot.json` and CRM adapter data instead of blocking.

## Product Overview

- **Product:** Collaborative warehouse robotics (picking arms, dock scanners, fleet navigation) plus onboarding/install packages
- **Category:** Industrial automation / warehouse robotics
- **One-liner:** Acme Robotics helps mid-market warehouses ship more with fewer picking errors via collaborative arms and dock sensing.

## Target Audience

- Mid-market manufacturers and 3PLs with 1–5 sites
- Operations and facilities leaders evaluating automation pilots

## Personas

- **Ops / facilities lead** — cares about uptime, install window, training burden
- **Economic buyer (VP Ops / COO)** — cares about payback period and risk
- **Champion (warehouse manager)** — needs a clean pilot story for leadership

## Problems & Pain Points

- Labor volatility on pick lines
- Dock congestion and blind spots
- Pilot quotes that stall waiting on formal numbers and security review

## Differentiation

- Packaged pilot kits (e.g. Brightline Line Pilot) with clear line items
- Onboarding + training SKUs bundled with hardware
- Human approval before any customer-facing send (sales ops discipline)

## Brand Voice

- Direct, operational, specific about SKUs and dates
- No hype, no invented proof points
- Prefer concrete next actions over vague “reach out soon”

## Proof Points

- Use only CRM-backed deal/activity facts in drafts
- Do not invent customer logos, win rates, or competitor prices

## Goals

- Daily sales-ops digest with pipeline hygiene signals
- Quote drafts that follow proposal structure (exec summary → solution → plan → investment → next steps)
- Every outbound email-shaped artifact gated for human approval

## Learnings

- Pilot week: mock CRM only; stub send log; no live HubSpot/email
- Prefer RevOps digest language for reports; sales-enablement proposal template for quotes
